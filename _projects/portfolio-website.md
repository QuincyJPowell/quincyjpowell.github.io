# Quincy Powell Personal Website

This website is an ongoing project to create a single place where the different parts of my work, education, interests, and creative life can exist together.

Rather than building a traditional portfolio focused on one professional specialty, I wanted to create a website that could grow with me. Academic research, professional work, writing, development, art, projects, and credentials each have their own place, while still being connected through a larger picture of who I am.

---

## Overview

The goal of this project is to build a personal website that is useful both as a professional resource and as a representation of me as a whole person.

The site needs to accommodate very different kinds of content without making any of them feel like an afterthought. A research project should be able to exist alongside a professional case study. A novel should be able to exist alongside a web development project. Personal writing should have a place without making the site feel like a personal blog.

That made the structure of the website itself an important part of the project.

---

## The Challenge

Traditional portfolio websites tend to organize everything around a single professional identity.

That approach works well when someone wants to present a narrow specialty, but it did not accurately represent the kind of work I wanted this website to hold.

My interests cross several areas, including:

- Academic research and analysis
- Digital marketing and SEO
- Writing and creative work
- Website and software development
- Art and visual work
- Long-term personal projects

The challenge was creating a structure that could accommodate those areas without turning the website into a collection of disconnected pages.

---

## Research & Inspiration

The structure of the website developed through research into information architecture, usability, accessibility, search engine optimization, and the ways personal websites organize complex bodies of work.

One of the most important conclusions was that the site's organization needed to reflect how a visitor understands the person, rather than how the underlying files happen to be organized.

This led to a distinction between **areas of work**, **projects**, and **credentials**.

Areas such as Academic, Professional, Writing, Development, and Art describe different parts of my life and work.

Projects represent individual pieces of work that may belong to one or more of those areas.

Credentials provide a formal record of qualifications and experience.

The website itself therefore acts as the layer connecting all three.

---

## Information Architecture

The current structure separates the major areas of the site while allowing individual projects to cross those boundaries when appropriate.

The primary sections are:

- About
- Academic
- Professional
- Writing
- Development
- Art
- Portfolio & Projects
- Credentials
- Contact

This structure is intentionally broad.

The goal is not to force every piece of content into a single category. Instead, it gives visitors several useful ways to discover the same body of work.

For example, a research project may belong to both Academic and Professional work. A development project may also involve substantial artwork or creative writing. A particularly important project can be featured in Portfolio & Projects without needing to be duplicated elsewhere.

---

## Content Architecture

Behind the visible navigation, the website uses a project-based content model.

Projects can be described using several kinds of metadata:

- **Project type** describes the area or areas of work a project belongs to.
- **Topics** describe what the project is about.
- **Format** describes the type of content.
- **Featured status** determines whether a project should be highlighted in curated areas.

This allows the website to maintain one canonical version of a project while making it discoverable from multiple relevant sections.

The goal is to avoid creating duplicate pages simply because a project crosses multiple areas of interest.

---

## Search Engine Optimization

SEO has been an important consideration throughout the development of the website.

Rather than treating SEO as a separate layer added after the site is built, I have approached it as part of the site's information architecture and content strategy.

The site structure is designed around clear page purposes, descriptive URLs, meaningful titles and descriptions, organized content, and relationships between related pieces of work.

This also makes the site easier for visitors to understand, which is ultimately more useful than optimizing pages around search engines alone.

---

## Accessibility

Accessibility is another consideration throughout the project.

The goal is to make the site understandable and usable across different devices, input methods, and levels of ability.

This includes considerations such as:

- Semantic HTML
- Clear heading structure
- Descriptive links
- Keyboard accessibility
- Responsive layouts
- Readable typography
- Appropriate contrast
- Meaningful alternative text for images
- Avoiding unnecessary visual or interaction barriers

Accessibility is treated as part of the design process rather than as a final checklist.

---

## Technical Implementation

The website is built with Jekyll and hosted through GitHub Pages.

The current architecture uses reusable layouts and includes, structured data files, and a dedicated project collection.

The project system is designed so that new work can be added without manually rebuilding every page that needs to reference it.

This makes the website easier to maintain as the amount and variety of content grows.

---

## Development Process

The website has evolved through multiple iterations rather than being designed as a finished product from the beginning.

Earlier versions were organized more closely around individual content categories and professional specialties. As the scope of the website expanded, that structure became increasingly restrictive.

The current version represents a shift toward a more flexible system that can accommodate the different directions my work may take over time.

The architecture is therefore being designed with future content in mind rather than only the material that exists today.

---

## What I Have Learned

Building my own website has become an opportunity to apply ideas from several different areas at once.

It has given me practical experience with:

- Information architecture
- Content strategy
- Search engine optimization
- Accessibility
- Responsive web design
- Jekyll and GitHub Pages
- HTML, CSS, Liquid, and YAML
- Content modeling
- Website maintenance
- Iterative design

More importantly, the project has changed how I think about websites.

A website is not simply a collection of pages. Its structure influences how people understand the information inside it, what they can find, and how they understand the person or organization behind it.

That makes the architecture itself part of the work.

---

## Current Status

This website is an ongoing project.

The underlying architecture, content model, visual system, and individual sections will continue to evolve as I add new academic work, professional projects, writing, development work, art, and other long-term projects.

The goal is not to create a website that is finished once and never changes.

The goal is to create a foundation that can keep changing with me.
